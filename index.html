The Navier–Stokes problem, solved by OpenAI

A 90-year-old maths problem was resolved in September 2026, not by one model, but by thousands of AI agents working in groups. This page explains how those agents were organised. No maths needed.

What happened, in short

  • OpenAI ran about 10,000 AI agents at once, split into groups.
  • Each group got the same problem, worded a different way. Agents could only talk inside their own group.
  • A small group of ~100 agents solved an easier, related problem first. That answer was handed to the big groups.
  • Humans did not do any maths. They steered: picked the wordings, moved compute around, and used Codex to pass the best ideas between groups.
  • The final proof was rewritten in a language a computer can check, line by line. It passed.
  • Total: 88 hours to the answer, 17 more to check it.

The question, in plain words

The Navier–Stokes equations describe how fluids move: water in a pipe, air over a wing. The open question was simple to state: can a perfectly smooth flow ever "break", meaning its speed shoots to infinity in a finite time?

OpenAI's agents showed that yes, it can, by building a concrete example where it happens. That is all the maths you need for this page.
